How Mock Board works.

A practice room you talk to. You speak, it listens to what you actually said, and it answers: a five-member UPSC board, a debate opponent, or a study partner. This page shows where everything is and how to get the most out of it.

[ 01 / What is where ]

Four screens, in order.

  1. 01HomeWhat it is and the models it uses.
  2. 02Choose and set upPick a mode, then a short form, a motion or a topic.
  3. 03The roomYou speak; the board answers out loud, with live captions.
  4. 04The reportHow you were heard, what to practise, and a second attempt.
  1. 1
    The status line. Tells you whose turn it is: the board is speaking, thinking, or it is your turn. Start talking when it says Your turn. You can also speak over the board to interrupt it.
  2. 2
    Live captions. Every line the board says, and what the speech service heard from you, as it happens. If your words come out wrong here, the board will see them that way too.
  3. 3
    The seats. The member who is speaking lights up. Each member has a different voice and a different area: your form, your district, your degree, your interests, and the situation question.
  4. 4
    The clock. Time used and the limit. The UPSC board starts to wrap up at 16 minutes and stops at 17. A debate stops at 10. The study partner has no wrap-up pressure.
  5. 5
    Where you are. The top line names the screen. The badge on the right shows the mode, and Demo mode when the server runs on stand-ins instead of the real models.
  6. 6
    End interview. Asks once, then ends and prepares the report. You can also end by voice (see section 03).

[ 02 / The three modes ]

Pick the practice you need today.

01

UPSC interview

A five-member board plans the sitting from your form (home state, degree, hobbies, optional, work) and asks one area each. It follows up on what you actually said, asks for a reason or an example when an answer is thin, and puts one real administrative situation to you, then raises the stakes once: “Now a local politician calls and asks you to send relief to their supporters first. What do you do?”

  • Four languages: Hinglish, English, Hindi, Tamil + English.
  • Take your time on big questions: pauses do not end your turn.
  • A microphone check before you start, in your language.
02

Debate

Choose a motion and your side. The board argues the other side: it opens, rebuts the points you actually made, cross-questions you, and closes. A judge gives a verdict with the exchanges it relied on.

  • Thirteen curated motions.
  • Two opponents: the Logician (calm, exact) or the Wit (quick, sharp).
  • Long turns for your opening and closing statements.
03

Study partner

Name a topic you are studying and explain it out loud, the way you would to a sharp friend. The partner restates your point so you know it was understood, then asks one good question: why, an example, or the strongest counterpoint. No scores, no timer pressure. You leave with your own points, made stronger.

  • Good for revision, and for a calmer session after a hard day.
  • Counterpoints are offered as “some would argue”, never as facts.
  • The summary keeps your own words.

[ 03 / Things you can say ]

Talk to the board like a person.

These phrases are caught by fixed rules, not by a model guessing. Every phrase below is checked by an automated test, and every Hinglish and English phrase, three Hindi phrases and one Tamil phrase were also recognised from real Gnani Prisma transcripts of generated (not human) speech. Rephrased requests are caught less often, and accuracy on real voices has not been measured yet.

These work with the UPSC board and the study partner; with the partner, “another topic” means leaving this point for your next one. A debate keeps its own clock and does not take spoken requests: use the End button. Ending by voice asks you to confirm once, out loud: say “Haan” or “Yes”. Saying anything else carries on. Asking for a moment to think costs no answer, and the same question stays.

[ 04 / The report ]

Feedback you can check.

How the board heard you

Every answer as the speech service wrote it, what the board understood, and what it did next. If a word was probably misheard, it says so beside your words: “क्राउड” was probably “Claude”. Your transcript is never edited.

Scores with quotes

Each score in the UPSC report quotes your own words as evidence, and the quote is checked against the transcript. No quote, no claim.

Practise this moment

Hear your weakest question again and answer it aloud. The page compares the two answers on simple, visible parts (a reason, who is affected, a first step, another side, a cost) without giving a mark.

[ 05 / Why not a general voice assistant ]

Narrow on purpose.

General voice assistants are excellent at open conversation. Mock Board does one job, practising for a spoken board, and is built around it.

[ 06 / Privacy and honesty ]

What happens to your words.

HearingGnani Prisma

Speech to text: writes down what you say.

VoiceGnani Timbre

Text to speech: each member's lines, spoken.

ReasoningAmazon Nova Pro

Chooses each next line, and writes the report.

  • Your speech goes to Gnani Prisma to be written down; the text goes to Amazon Bedrock (Nova Pro, Sydney region) so the board can answer.
  • The server keeps a text record of the sitting so faults can be found. Audio is not saved unless the room shows a recording notice.
  • The board members are fictional. Mock Board is a student project for Gnani.ai's Great Indian AI Internship Challenge. It is not run by or affiliated with the Union Public Service Commission, and a report from it is not a UPSC mark.
  • Known limits: Tamil wording has not yet been reviewed by a fluent speaker, and accuracy on real accents has not been measured at scale.
