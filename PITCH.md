# Mock Board: a UPSC interview board with ears, a voice and a spine

**In one line:** a voice-first mock UPSC Personality Test where five AI board members hear you in
Hinglish through **Gnani Prisma**, speak in their own voices through **Gnani Timbre**, and push back
like a real board. They hold you to your own words, cut in on a false claim, and never press an
honest "I don't know".

**The problem.** The interview is the hardest stage of UPSC to practise. Good mock boards are
costly and few, mostly in big cities and mostly in English, while many candidates think and speak
in Hinglish or Hindi. Text chatbots don't train the real skill: answering out loud, under pressure,
to a panel that listens.

**The solution.** Fill a short, fictional form. The board prepares from it live: each member gets
lines of questioning and a weak spot to probe. The sitting follows a real board's order
(introduction, degree subject, a case study dug into, home district, hobbies). Then you get a
report on the qualities UPSC looks for, with your own words as evidence.

**How it works**
- **Ears and voice:** Gnani Prisma v2.5 streams speech-to-text while you speak. Gnani Timbre v2.5
  gives each member a distinct voice, and Hindi lines are spoken from Devanagari so they sound
  native.
- **The model proposes, code decides:** an LLM (Amazon Nova Pro on Bedrock, or your own Claude
  over MCP) suggests each move: dig, elaborate, confront, pivot. A deterministic director allows
  or refuses it with a reason. A confrontation must quote words Prisma actually heard.
- **Real-time like a real panel:** live transcripts let the board cut in on a false claim, nudge
  ("point par aaiye"), or stop when you talk over it, remembering what it hadn't said yet. It
  decides the moment you stop, and a short "हम्म" fills the pause.
- **An evidence-based report:** every quote in the report is checked against the transcript.

**What is new:** an auditable referee between the LLM and the candidate; fairness rules in code
(equal dignity on communal remarks, no pressure on an honest unknown); voice turn-taking built for
Indian languages; and bring-your-own-AI over a 14-tool MCP server, so the person's own model does
the reasoning.

**Proof, measured and honest:** 3,684 automated tests, including board-behaviour invariants.
Live sittings ran on Nova Pro and on Claude. The wait from the end of an answer to the board's
reasoned reply fell from 12–16 s to about 5 s (median, scripted runs), and the reaction plays at
once. Limits: Tamil is not yet reviewed by a fluent speaker, all data is synthetic, and real fact
cards are not written yet.

**Who it is for:** aspirants practising alone, coaching institutes that need mock boards at
scale, and EdTech products that can plug it in over MCP. It also includes Mitra, a study partner
on any topic, and a debate mode.

**Built with:** Gnani Prisma v2.5 · Gnani Timbre v2.5 · Amazon Nova Pro (Bedrock) · Claude (MCP,
Agent SDK) · Pipecat · LangGraph · Python.
